# Round 3 research

Findings behind the three directions. Every row in `research.json` carries an evidence label: observed-live, vendor-page, third-party or inferred. Vendor conversion claims are treated as claims, not evidence.

## What Ratestar teaches

- The owner's reference screenshot is Ratestar's own marketing demo (Maxi Park Hotel & Spa, Velingrad, from its mockup video), not a neutral client example. It shows the vendor's flagship template, which is reused across its hotel, restaurant and beauty pages.
- Strength to beat: a strong sense of place. The hero film shows the property's physical sign, and the photo cards make each destination feel tangible. RepKey has to match that feeling without photography: a wordmark system built from displayName, tonal fields from the brand's own hex values, grain, and patterns or frames. Owned photography comes later.
- Strength to beat: the review action sits above the fold at 375x812 even on a long hub page. RepKey should go further and put the whole rating block (question, stars, submit) in the first screen at 375x667 with the Safari toolbars showing, with nothing above it except identity.
- Strength to beat: useful follow-on content (booking, spa, menu, map, contact) and 'same QR, new content'. RepKey's stable tokens plus draft, publish and rollback are stronger than immediate edits. Its link rows should be calmer, capped at four visible, and grouped.
- Strength to beat: Ratestar sets the 'premium' bar in the BG market (InterContinental Sofia, Therma Numera, Maxi Park). Owners will compare RepKey against it side by side, so the beta must look finished, not like a form.
- Weakness to avoid: public platforms come first and the portal page has no private step. The 'Google Reviews' label opens a Ratestar form on some portals and Google directly on others, and Tripadvisor is sometimes direct. The CTA label does not say what happens next.
- Weakness to avoid (from the shipped JS, not exercised live): ratings above nrpThreshold (default 3) auto-redirect to the platform after 200ms. The public invite can be limited to 3 stars or more. The back button is trapped with pushState. Confetti fires on every submit, a '98% of customers trust reviews' nudge appears, and a click is recorded as a 'converted' review. That contradicts its own 'we don't gate' FAQ. RepKey should show its fairness in the UI (identical Google card), not just claim it.
- Weakness to avoid: five filled gold emoji stars inside the 'Google' and 'Tripadvisor' cards prime the expected score, and they break Google's and Tripadvisor's brand guidance (no stars next to the Google name; use Tripadvisor bubbles only as supplied).
- Weakness to avoid: a self-issued blue 'verified' seal next to the property name imitates platform verification. RepKey must never use seals or Google-like styling.
- Weakness to avoid: emoji used as UI (pin, phone, fleur-de-lis, pointing hands, vote boxes), a crowded centred hierarchy, the property name repeated twice, and off-mission blocks (award-vote CTA, discount promo) competing with the review moment.
- Weakness to avoid: the property's brand colours are ignored. Maxi Park's site uses gold #AA8453 and Cormorant Garamond; the portal is generic black with Montserrat. RepKey should derive the page from the property's own colours and use a Cyrillic-correct, curated type pairing, so two properties never look like the same template.
- Weakness to avoid: the language pill swaps only the UI chrome, so there are /en pages in Bulgarian and /bg pages in English, and it uses a flag. RepKey should use native-name text controls and translated content per locale (including link labels), and flag missing translations before publish.
- Weakness to avoid: a heavy autoplay mp4 hero served from storage costs LCP and roaming data on hotel Wi-Fi, and failed images leave black boxes. RepKey's no-photo variant should stay under about 150KB and render server-side instantly.
- Weakness to avoid: no privacy notice on the portal, public indexing (robots follow, index), live typos on 5-star client pages ('INTERCONTINTENTAL'), gamification sold on higher tiers (scratch card, wheel of fortune, prizes), staff review leaderboards, and device fingerprinting. RepKey's noindex, first-party, no-tracker, no-incentive posture should be visible in one line on the page.
- Sales takeaway: hotel buyers arrive expecting gating as a feature, because NFC and QR hardware vendors openly sell 'filtration'. RepKey's admin onboarding needs a short, confident explanation that equal access is the Google-compliant choice that protects their public profile (GBP can stop new reviews and show warnings for fake engagement).

## Opportunities

- Be the only premium portal in the BG/EU market that visibly guarantees equal Google access: the same card after 1 and after 5 stars. Pitch it as the choice that protects the owner's Google profile. Show it in the admin preview by putting the low-rating and high-rating after-rating states side by side.
- Own the no-photo state. No competitor designs one. Launch the beta with typographic, tonal and pattern identities that owners are proud of, so the upload block reads as a feature, not a gap.
- Do Bulgarian typography right: self-hosted OFL faces with Bulgarian locl forms (Cormorant Garamond, Playfair 2, Ysabeau, Sofia Sans, Manrope), lang on the page root and per span, and Cyrillic-aware wordmark sizing. Ratestar, Satoshi and most vendors render BG in Russian forms or system fallbacks.
- Ship a first screen with no banner and no trackers: remove the non-exempt sessionStorage dedupe (subject to counsel), move the disclosure inline under the submit button, and stop loading fonts from third-party CDNs on /p. This is calmer and faster than Trustpilot, Linktree or NiceJob.
- Make the handoff to Google honest. Tell guests Google may ask them to sign in, record only the selection, and never thank them for a review. The receipt copy becomes a trust asset against vendors who claim 'Thanks for your review'.
- Turn guest rights into a quiet premium feature: one-line receipt with Change, a 'Your response' disclosure with local-time deadlines, an inline two-step withdraw, and a calm shared-device reset. Competitors either allow no edits (Bolt, MENU TIGER) or bury them.
- Localize fully, not just the chrome: localized link labels and category titles (a new capability), missing-translation checks before publish, and a bilingual, brand-free unavailable page chosen by Accept-Language. This fixes Ratestar's mixed-language pages.
- Derive each page from the property's own brand with contrast guarantees: automatic stage polarity, derived accent roles, and a logo plate. Ratestar ignores brand colours, and Touch Stay and Duve offer pickers with only a contrast tip.
- Sell durability: stable QR and NFC tokens, draft and published snapshots, rollback, and 'publish changes while live'. Compare against ReviewPro's irreversible 'Save & Publish' and against link-in-bio platforms that died with printed codes (Koji 2024, Tap Bio 31 Oct 2026).
- Offer premium print collateral per composition (table tent, reception card, spa card, NFC card copy). Google's branded review kit is reportedly gone, and owners want objects that match their interior.
- Hit a performance budget competitors miss: under about 150KB, no video, two font files, server-rendered first paint, LCP well under 2.5s on congested hotel Wi-Fi and in in-app QR browsers.
- Report honestly as a positioning point with GMs: 'Guests who opened Google', never 'reviews collected'. No staff leaderboards or quotas (banned by Google and denied by RepKey). Availability by exception and a 10-rating comparison floor.
- Support multi-placement properties well: one property identity, many placement portals (reception, spa, restaurant, pool bar) with local wording, portal groups and per-placement print pieces. Competitors mostly offer one page per location.
- Be the calm alternative to the dense, emoji-heavy BG link-hub aesthetic: one question, one accent, real icons, at most 4 visible links. That contrast alone reads as premium next to Ratestar in a sales demo.
- Fix today's visible defects as quick wins before the redesign ships: no selected-star state, policy copy shown to guests, app dark-theme leak on /p, UTC deadlines, and the bottom bar over the CTA.

## Photography in the beta

Beta photo strategy: design every direction to be finished with no photograph, then add owned photography as a later layer that plugs into a slot each composition already defines.

Tier 0 ships now and needs no uploads. Identity comes from:

- displayName set by wordmark rules: auto-fit, a 2-line layout up to 35+ characters, and a separate size cap for Cyrillic names (about 22-26px on one line at 320px).
- An optional monogram seal of the initials, which works for both Latin and Cyrillic names.
- Tonal fields derived from the three brand hex values by shifting OKLab lightness only.
- A pre-rendered grain tile of about 2KB at 4-8% opacity. Do not use a live feTurbulence filter.
- For C6, a curated library of small SVG place patterns (mountain contours, sea lines, geometric weave, ink marbling, vineyard rows, stone), tinted by the brand.
- Architectural frame shapes that hold colour today and a photo later.

Tier 0 also covers the historical hero_image_url. It must render only inside the composition's photo slot, with explicit dimensions, object-fit cover, a scrim into the surface so text never sits on a raw photo, a solid-colour placeholder, and fetchpriority=high only when it is the LCP element. og:image keeps using it; new portals could later get a server-rendered typographic share card.

Do not use Google Business Profile photos:

- The GBP APIs are scoped to creating, managing and reporting on listings.
- They forbid caching beyond 30 days and any manipulation or aggregation, and cropping, tinting or overlaying is arguably manipulation.
- googleUrl values change over time.
- Customer media carries attribution that must not be altered and belongs to guests.
- Tools must not replicate the Business Profile look or imply endorsement.

Places API Place Photos is lawful but a poor fit. It is billable, needs Google Maps attribution plus an author credit and link on every photo, allows no storage, returns uncontrolled user-contributed images, and puts Google branding onto a non-Google experience. If photo reuse matters commercially, ask Google in writing through the existing correspondence in docs/external/google. The default answer is no.

Do not use stock photography either: Birdeye's stock banners show that stock reads as 'not our place'.

Tier 1 comes after the SAFE-01 upload path. The property uploads its own originals, which it owns or licenses; uploading to Google does not transfer ownership. The upload needs:

- A rights attestation and a takedown path.
- Moderation, EXIF stripping and a focal point.
- Server-side resizing to AVIF/WebP at two widths, capped at 2x the rendered size.
- One photo per property first, with per-placement overrides later.
- Wiring the organisation logo from Identity into portals, with a luminance check that decides stage polarity or a logo plate.

Slot rules per composition:

- C1: the atmosphere band, 26-34svh or less, with a scrim.
- C2: a small 4:5 inset plate beside the standfirst, never full-bleed.
- C3: optional duotone inside the poster, tinted to primary.
- C4: the card header band at 16:7.
- C5: none.
- C6: inside the frame.

No carousels, no video and no autoplay at any tier. Printed collateral always uses the Tier 0 identity, so the printed code never depends on a photo.

### Can the portal use a property's Google Business Profile photos?

Short answer: do not use the Business Profile APIs as the photo source for the guest portal. There are two lawful routes. (1) The property supplies its own original files, which it owns or has licensed; this is a future capability, since upload is safety-blocked in the beta. (2) Places API Place Photos, displayed live with strict attribution. For the beta, design with no photo.

Details:
(a) Business Profile APIs (Media: COVER, PROFILE/LOGO and category media via accounts.locations.media). The policies limit use to 'create, manage, and report on business listings that you either own or are authorized to manage'. Rendering a hotel's cover photo on a public guest page is not managing or reporting on the listing, so it is very likely outside the permitted scope. My confidence is medium because Google does not name this case explicitly. Other constraints:

- Content may not be pre-fetched, cached or stored except temporarily (30 days or less) and 'cannot be manipulated or aggregated in any way'. Cropping, tinting or overlaying a hero image is arguably manipulation.
- googleUrl 'is not static since it may change over time', so hotlinking will break.
- Customer media carries an Attribution object that 'You must display ... and must not delete or alter'. Those photos belong to guests, not the property.
- Tools must not replicate the Business Profile look and feel or imply Google endorsement.
  RepKey already corresponds with Google about GBP data use (docs/external/google/). If photo reuse matters commercially, ask Google in writing rather than infer permission.
  Sources: https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/policies (updated 2026-08-28); https://developers.google.com/my-business/reference/rest/v4/accounts.locations.media

(b) Google Maps Platform Places API (New), Place Photos. Display without a map is allowed, with conditions:

- The Google Maps logo or text attribution must appear.
- Every photo must credit its author (avatar, name, profile link), and users must be able to open the source on Google Maps (googleMapsUri).
- Nothing except place IDs may be cached or stored.
- Requests are billable.

### QR and NFC practice

Stable URLs

- Print one opaque, high-entropy token URL per placement on a RepKey-controlled HTTPS domain (e.g. https://rk.example/p/Ab3xK9), matching ADR 0044's keyed-hash tokens.
- Include no personal data, no room numbers and no tracking query strings.
- Printed codes must survive content edits, republishing and rollback: the token resolves to the currently published snapshot. W3C's principle applies: 'Cool URIs don't change.'
- Revoking or rotating a token should show a gentle page ('This code is no longer active – [Property]') instead of a 404, with a grace period for rotation.
- Do not put third-party URL shorteners in print.
- Print the short URL in text under every code as a fallback. This also serves accessibility and phones without a scanner.

QR (DENSO WAVE / ISO/IEC 18004)

- Keep a 4-module quiet zone on all sides. Design templates so borders, text and illustrations never touch it.
- Use dark modules on a light background with strong contrast. For dark premium designs, place the code on a light plate; inverted or light-on-dark codes fail on some readers (medium confidence).
- Error correction M by default. Use Q or H when a centre logo is added (logo no more than about 20% of the area, never over finder patterns) or for table tents that get dirty.
- Shorter URLs give lower-version codes with bigger modules at the same print size.
- Rule of thumb, an industry heuristic with medium confidence: code width ≈ scanning distance ÷ 10, and at least about 2–2.5 cm for hand-held cards.
- Use a matte finish because glossy lamination glares under lobby lights. Avoid curved surfaces.
- Test on older iPhones and Androids in low light before printing.
- Add a clear call to action with an honest scope, e.g. 'Rate your visit – private, about 30 seconds'. Do not write 'Review us on Google 5★', which is a Google brand and gating problem, not only a style one.
- Anti-tamper ('quishing'): use tamper-evident mounting. The landing page should show the property name and the RepKey domain prominently so guests can check where they landed. The portal never asks for credentials or payment, and the copy can say so.

NFC (Apple Core NFC; Android NFC basics)

- Write one NDEF message whose first record is a URI record (TNF Well-Known, type 'U') with the same https token URL. iOS uses the first URI record; Android dispatches on the first record. Custom schemes do not work in iOS background reading.
- Use widely compatible NFC Forum Type 2 tags, e.g. NTAG213/215/216 (industry practice, medium confidence).
- Permanently lock tags after writing. Public, unlocked tags can be rewritten to a phishing URL.
- iPhone XS and later read tags in the background only while in use. Not available when the device has never been unlocked, the camera is open, Apple Pay Wallet is active, a Core NFC session is running, or airplane mode is on. A notification appears and the guest must tap it before Safari opens.
- Android reads when the screen is unlocked and NFC is on. From Android 16, http/https tags open via ACTION_VIEW. From Android 17 an 'open link' notification needs an explicit tap.
- Card copy: 'Hold the top of your phone to the card, then tap the link.' Always combine NFC with a QR code and the printed URL.
- Place the tag away from metal (or use on-metal tags), and mark the tap spot visually.

Measurement

- Count a scan only server-side when the page is actually requested by a browser. Filter link-preview bots and prefetchers so qualified_scans stays honest (already in ADR 0044).
- Never add per-guest identifiers to printed URLs.

### Accessibility obligations

Obligation: under the EAA (Directive 2019/882, and in Bulgaria the Закон за изискванията за достъпност на продукти и услуги, in force 28.06.2025), a no-login feedback portal that concludes no consumer contract is probably not an 'e-commerce service' (Art. 3(30)). The hotel's booking engine reached from the link tree is in scope in its own right. Hotels that are microenterprises are exempt for services (Art. 4(5)). Medium confidence; counsel should confirm per customer, especially if a portal ever embeds booking or ordering. In the US, hotels and restaurants are ADA Title III public accommodations, and DOJ treats their web content as covered; that is low-to-medium relevance for the beta. Recommendation: treat WCAG 2.2 AA as the design bar. EN 301 549 v4.1.1 (ETSI, 2 Sep 2026) adopts WCAG 2.2 AA and maps it to the EAA. It is not yet cited in the Official Journal, so v3.2.1 (WCAG 2.1 AA) remains the formal reference for now.

WCAG 2.2 AA essentials for this UI:

1. Star rating (1.3.1, 4.1.2, 2.5.3, 1.4.1)

- Build it as a native radio group in a fieldset with a visible question as the legend.
- Each star gets an accessible name with a word, such as '1 of 5, Poor' up to '5 of 5, Excellent'. Show the word visibly on selection.
- Arrow keys move between stars.
- Selection must not rely on colour alone. Use a filled versus outline shape plus the text label.
- All stars start unselected.

2. No auto-submit on star tap (3.2.2 On Input). Keep an explicit Submit button, which the current UI already has. Activate on pointer-up so a press can be cancelled (2.5.2). No slider or swipe-only rating (2.5.7).
3. Target size (2.5.8): at least 24×24 CSS px is the legal floor. Design stars, language switch, link-tree rows, receipt actions and the privacy link at about 44–48 px. Leave spacing between the five stars so a mis-tap does not select a neighbour.
4. Contrast (1.4.3, 1.4.11)

- Body text needs 4.5:1; large text (24 px, or 18.66 px bold) needs 3:1.
- Star outlines, input borders, focus rings and the unselected-versus-selected difference need 3:1.
- Champagne or gold on ivory, and mid-grey on ink, commonly fail. Validate every theme token pair, and let admins pick only palettes that pass.
- Text over a hero photo needs a scrim or solid plate. Assume a worst-case image.

5. Focus (2.4.7, 2.4.11): a visible, designed focus ring on every control. Sticky headers, language bars and bottom docks must not cover the focused element.
6. Reflow and text size (1.4.10, 1.4.4, 1.4.12, 1.3.4)

- Works at 320 CSS px with no horizontal scroll.
- Supports 200% text and user text spacing without clipping, so avoid fixed-height cards.
- Portrait and landscape both work.

7. Language (3.1.1, 3.1.2)

- Set lang="bg" or lang="en" on html.
- Mark language-switch labels with their own lang ('Български', 'English').
- Label the switch with language names, not flags only. Flags are a best practice issue rather than a WCAG failure, but they denote countries, not languages.

8. Motion and media (2.2.2, 2.3.1, 1.4.2)

- An auto-playing hero video like Ratestar's needs a pause control if it runs longer than 5 s, and no autoplay audio.
- Honour prefers-reduced-motion for entrance, star and receipt animations. Never flash.

9. Text alternatives and structure (1.1.1, 2.4.2, 2.4.6, 2.4.4)

- The logo image's alt text is the property name; decorative imagery uses alt="".
- Page title like 'Rate your visit – [Property]'.
- One h1 plus logical headings and landmarks.
- Link text says where it goes: 'Write a review on Google (opens Google Maps)'. Mark external links and new-window behaviour.

10. Feedback form (3.3.1, 3.3.2, 4.1.3)

- The optional private-feedback textarea has a persistent label, not a placeholder-only label, a visible 'optional' marker and a live character count.
- Errors are identified in text.
- Submission success and the receipt are announced through a status message without moving focus unexpectedly.

11. Redundant Entry (3.3.7): the correct-or-withdraw flow pre-fills the guest's earlier rating and text instead of asking again.
12. Consistent Help (3.2.6): the privacy and help link stays in the same place across arrival, rating, receipt and error states.
13. Timing (2.2.1): if a correction window or session expires, say so and explain what happens. No silent timeouts on a draft.
14. Accessible Authentication (3.3.8) is satisfied by design, since there is no login and no CAPTCHA. Keep it that way; use invisible abuse controls rather than puzzles.
15. Print: the printed QR/NFC card is not WCAG content, but give it a large human-readable short URL, high contrast and a plain-language call to action. That also serves guests who cannot scan.

Patterns across these products:

Patterns across these products:

- Rating-first surfaces are one question per screen with large targets: Trustpilot's five 46px tiles, Trustmary's single card, Birdeye's stars then Next. Almost all leave the lower half of the phone empty. A premium hospitality portal can use that space for atmosphere (brand colour field, typography, optional photo) instead of blank white.
- Word labels for star values (Trustpilot: Bad / Poor / Average / Great / Excellent) and end-point legends (Trustmary) are the accessible norm. Few vendors show the chosen word visibly; RepKey can, in EN and BG.
- Business identity is usually weak or overshadowed by the vendor: vendor logo headers (Trustmary), 'Powered by' floating badges (NiceJob, Senja), vendor adverts on the completion page (Podium). A portal where the property brand leads and RepKey is a quiet footer mark is a clear differentiator.

- Personal data is commonly demanded: name and email required (Testimonial.to), callback requests (Birdeye), phone for promos (Podium). RepKey's 'no personal details' stance can be said at the action as reassurance microcopy, as Senja does with its sharing-control line.
- Third-party tracking and cookie walls are common on review pages: Trustpilot's cookie dialog plus reCAPTCHA device signals, and NiceJob loading GTM, Google Analytics and the Facebook SDK. RepKey's first-party server-side telemetry means no banner, a faster and calmer first screen.

Patterns across these products:

- First-interaction speed has two fast archetypes. (a) One question with a language-free scale as the first thing on screen (HappyOrNot: one question, four faces, done in three taps; sunday and me&u: a star row straight after payment). (b) A link hub with zero steps (Linktree, Beacons: the first screen is logo, name and 4-6 buttons). The Ratestar reference does neither: a hero video, contacts, social icons and a paragraph all come before any action. For RepKey, keep the private star row inside the first 812px screen, and let a compact link row sit below it from arrival.
- Rating inputs seen: 5 stars (sunday, me&u, OpenTable, Resy, Toast handheld), thumbs (Toast receipts, Toast Pay per dish), 4 faces with no neutral (HappyOrNot), out of 10 (TheFork), one-tap or Likert (MENU TIGER). Stars dominate wherever the next step is Google. That is also a risk, because guests can mistake private stars for the Google rating (sunday even prefills Google with them). RepKey should label the row visibly as private to the venue and never carry the score over to Google.
- Review gating is the default in restaurant tools, not the exception. me&u (configurable minimum star rating, on by default), sunday (satisfied guests get a Google button, dissatisfied ones a private channel), Tablein ('If the review is very high' the guest is invited to Google, Tripadvisor or Facebook), and HappyOrNot Smiley Sign (positive responders to a reviews page, concerns to a contact form). Only the tools that never route to Google avoid it (OpenTable, Resy, Toast Pay). Rules they conflict with: Google Maps policy ('selectively solicit positive reviews'; no pressure 'while on the premises'; no incentives), Tripadvisor ('the user interface and experience for submitting positive and negative reviews must be identical'), Yelp (don't ask at all, and not after a survey), and EU UCPD Annex I 23b/23c plus Art. 7(6). RepKey's 'Google for every guest' plus 'private feedback additionally at or below the threshold' is a real, sellable differentiator: pitch it to owners as the version that cannot get their profile flagged.
- OpenTable's form is the best compliant pattern: the public review and a 'confidential note to the restaurant' sit in one flow that is the same for every score. In RepKey terms, after rating show the same Google action at the same size and position for every score, and show private feedback as an added option below it, never as a replacement or a detour.
- Venue identity in these tools uses at most three levers: a logo disc or wordmark, one background or accent colour (Riviera: olive-black; Incontro: deep teal and terracotta; me&u Market House: burgundy rgb(93,9,35) on white), and one photo (me&u header 375x131, Beacons wallpaper). Add a one-line voice descriptor ('Open 11:30 - LATE', 'A tradição Italiana no Porto', 'Dogs welcome, people can come too'). Typography almost never belongs to the venue (Link Sans, Manrope, Poppins). Curated type pairings per property would immediately look more premium than every restaurant tool in this set, and would carry the no-photo requirement.
- Link-hub ergonomics converge on full-width stacked buttons 56-64px tall with 12-16px gaps, a centred or icon-left label, and Menu or Reservations first, then Website, Maps or Location, then WhatsApp or Instagram. None of the observed tools groups links into categories. Emoji often stand in for icons (Beacons, Ratestar). RepKey's ordered categories can use real line icons and short secondary lines (for example 'Menu · Today until 23:00').
- Post-rating and thank-you states in payment flows stack tip, rating, Google or feedback, loyalty and thank-you. The one element that feels premium is the venue-voice closing line (sunday: 'It was a pleasure cooking for you tonight! You're always welcome here.'). For RepKey the completion state should be a warm venue-authored sentence, the same Google action, and a quiet receipt area (correct, withdraw, reset device), with no promos or perks.
- Low-rating handling across the set: instant staff alerts (Toast SMS with 'negative only' / 'all' / 'none' presets; MENU TIGER; HappyOrNot volume-based alerts; me&u Crew 'Fast Feedback' so staff can fix it in-venue); a morning digest (Resy, sunday); a weekly email (Toast Menu Item Review, Thursdays); AI theme summaries (Toast: 'Atmosphere, Service, Food Quality, Wait Time'; sunday: Food & Drink, Service Quality, Ambiance, Value for Money); context on each item (table, server, seated time). For RepKey's admin phase: notify responsible managers at or below the threshold, digest by Portal and Portal Group, and use the portal or placement as the context field.

- CITATION CORRECTION (verified in the primary consolidated texts): the Bulgarian transposition of ePrivacy Art. 5(3) is Закон за електронната търговия (ZET) чл. 4а (new, ДВ бр. 105/2011, in force 29.12.2011; ал.1 т.1 amended ДВ бр. 17/2019), not ЗЕС чл. 4а. - ZET чл. 4а as written. ал.1: a provider may store or access information in the recipient's terminal only if (1) it gives clear and comprehensive information under GDPR Art. 13 and (2) the recipient has the option to REFUSE. ал.2: information about stored data must be available at any time. ал.3: later storage by the same provider does not re-trigger ал.1 if the recipient has not objected. ал.4: ал.1 does NOT apply where storage or access is 'necessary' for (1) transmitting a communication or (2) providing an information-society service explicitly requested by the recipient. The literal Bulgarian regime is inform-plus-opt-out, lighter than the directive's 'consent'. EU-conform reading after CJEU Planet49 (C-673/17, from prior knowledge) treats non-exempt storage as opt-in. For exempt storage, both the consent and the ал.1 information duty fall away.
- Enforcement: ZET чл. 20 gives supervision to the Комисия за защита на потребителите (КЗП), not КЗЛД. ZET чл. 24 fines are 250-1500 лв (individual) or 500-2000 лв (company), and 500-2500 / 1000-4000 лв for repeats. КЗЛД remains the authority for the GDPR side (Art. 13 transparency, lawful basis). I found no КЗЛД cookie-specific guidance within the call budget; that is still a gap.
- EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of Art. 5(3), v2.0 adopted 7 Oct 2024 (read in full from the EDPB PDF). Para 42: routing identifiers such as the IP address and session identifiers can fall under Art. 5(3). Para 55: 'gaining access to IP addresses would only trigger the application of Article 5(3) ePD in cases where this information originates from the terminal equipment' (static IPv4, IPv6); unless the entity can show otherwise, it must follow Art. 5(3). Para 56: applicability 'does not systematically mean that consent needs to be collected'; exemptions must be assessed case by case. Consequence: the IP-HMAC 'network marker' is safest framed and used as security or abuse protection, not as the visit counter.
- CNIL, the most concrete EU guidance fetched: exempt trackers include authentication, security and anti-bot, user-interface preferences, load balancing, remembering the cookie choice, and audience measurement under conditions ('que dit la loi', 29 Sep 2020). The audience-measurement exemption (page updated 4 Jul 2025) requires a purpose strictly limited to the publisher's own audience measurement, anonymous statistics only, no cross-referencing with other processing, and no cross-site tracking. It recommends a 13-month tracker life and 25-month data retention, and says users should be informed, for example through the privacy policy, not through a banner. Bulgaria's ZET 4а(4) has no equivalent analytics carve-out.
- Classification of RepKey's device-side items (from source): (a) rk_guest_session (24h, HttpOnly, CSRF plus response integrity, correction, withdrawal, shared-device reset) falls within 'explicitly requested service' and security, so it is exempt. (b) The localStorage acknowledgement flag works like CNIL's 'remember the choice' tracker and is exempt; it disappears if the bar does. (c) The sessionStorage 'guest-scan-recorded:*' marker exists only to dedupe the qualified_scans metric. It is the one item not squarely strictly necessary for the guest-requested service, and under a literal ZET reading it needs information plus a refusal option. (d) The network HMAC is server-side, with nothing stored on the device; it is in scope for Art. 5(3) only via EDPB para 55. Recommendation for counsel: remove (c) and dedupe the visit server-side, keep (d) scoped to abuse pressure, and have the count be 'portal page served to a signed session' on the server. Then everything on the device is exempt and no banner is needed. Caveat, from CNIL's multi-purpose rule (prior knowledge, not re-fetched): reusing rk_guest_session as the dedupe key mixes an exempt and a measurement purpose, so counsel should confirm the count itself qualifies as exempt, CNIL-style anonymous first-party measurement.
- ANSWER TO THE GAP: an inline, non-blocking sentence is sufficient once all device storage is exempt. Neither ePD 5(3) nor ZET 4а imposes a notice duty for exempt storage. GDPR Art. 12/13 requires concise, easily accessible information at collection, which a one-line pointer next to the rating submit plus a proper /privacy section satisfies (layered-notice approach from the WP29/EDPB transparency guidelines WP260, prior knowledge). A dismissible overlay is only warranted if the product keeps non-exempt storage, and then it must offer a real refusal, which the current 'Got it' does not. The current bar is therefore the worst of both.
- Required privacy-notice change (docs/legal/privacy-notice.md): add a 'Cookies and device storage' table listing rk_guest_session (purpose, 24h, strictly necessary), each browser-storage key and its purpose and lifetime, and the server-side network pseudonym (7-day expiry, abuse protection only). Today the guest section never uses the word 'cookie' and omits the storage keys.

### Brand and content model

All paths are relative to /Users/bozhidardenev/kodes/worktrees/rk-portal-redesign (current main, 878268bf1). There are two branding layers, and only one of them reaches guests.

(1) PROPERTY BRAND PROFILE, one per Property. This is what guests see. It lives in src/contexts/portal/application/ports/portal-experience.repository.ts and domain/portal-publication-snapshot.ts (PortalBrandProfileSnapshot).

- Fields: displayName (1–120), logoUrl, defaultHeroImageUrl, primaryColor, backgroundColor, textColor (each colour is a 6-digit hex), and version.
- Colour validation is in portal-experience.dto.ts. Text/background contrast must be at least 4.5:1. primary/background is only checked to be parseable, with no contrast floor.
- Only an AccountAdmin (portal.admin) can edit it. The save path is savePropertyPortalBrandProfile in use-cases/manage-portal-experience.ts. It hard-codes logoUrl and defaultHeroImageUrl to null, so a manager cannot set a URL.
- Default palette is #2563EB / #FFFFFF / #111827 (DEFAULT_PROPERTY_BRAND_PALETTE).
- displayName is also the AI-reply business name. It is edited in Property settings → Profile (property-public-display-name-card.tsx) and in the setup wizard. The portal editor only links to it.

(2) PROPERTY LOCALIZED CONTENT, per Property and locale: title (1–120) and shortDescription (1–500). Both are required for every enabled locale before publish. AccountAdmin only. UI label: 'Property-wide fallback'.

(3) PORTAL LOCALIZED OVERRIDE, per Portal and locale: title (≤120 or null) and shortDescription (≤500 or null). A null falls back to the Property content. heroImageUrl exists but is forced to null. Editable with portal.update.

(4) PORTAL ENTITY (domain/types.ts):

- name (≤100, internal/admin label), slug (2–64; there is no public slug route, URLs are /p/{token}), description (≤500, legacy/internal), heroImageUrl (nullable, historical only).
- theme {primaryColor, backgroundColor?, textColor?}. This is a per-Portal legacy palette, edited with ThemePresetSelector: Light #6366f1/#fff/#111827, Dark #a5b4fc/#111827/#f9fafb, Brand #b45309/#fffbeb/#1c1917, and Custom with a primary colour picker only.
- privateFeedbackThreshold (1–5, default 3), publicationState (draft|published|disabled|archived), primaryGuestLocale (en|bg), additionalGuestLocales (max 1).

Publication and resolution (repositories/portal-publication.repository.ts, infrastructure/portal-command-store.ts, use-cases/resolve-public-portal-token.ts):

- Every new publish builds a schema-v2 snapshot. Publishing is refused unless the brand profile exists and every enabled locale has content.
- The guest-facing values are resolved as follows. h1 = override title, falling back to Property title. Description = override shortDescription, falling back to Property shortDescription. The muted line under the title is brandProfile.displayName. Logo = brand logoUrl, which is null today. Hero = override hero, then brand default hero, which is null today. Theme colours = brand profile colours, which OVERRIDE portal.theme entirely.
- Consequence: the per-Portal Theme presets, portal.name and portal.description change nothing on a live v2 portal. They drive only the admin list swatch and the admin preview.

Rendering (portal-theme-style.ts): CSS variables --portal-primary, --portal-on-primary (black or white chosen by WCAG luminance in portal-contrast.ts), --portal-bg, --portal-text, --portal-accent-soft (12% primary), --portal-accent-border (40% primary), --portal-text-muted (72% text mixed into bg).

What an admin cannot set today:

- No fonts, layout or template choice, no radius/style options.
- No logo or photo: upload is safety-blocked and URLs are forced null. The organization logo upload in Identity is not wired to portals.
- No per-portal colours on v2.
- No custom guest UI copy: the question, button labels and receipts come from an immutable language pack (guest-ui-en-v1 / guest-ui-bg-v1) that the snapshot pins.
- No placement/channel label, no social icons, no address/phone.

### Secondary links

Data model (domain/types.ts, dto/portal-link*.dto.ts):

- A Portal has ordered PortalLinkCategory rows: title 1–100, sortKey. Each category has ordered PortalLink rows: label 1–100, url https-only ≤500, iconKey ≤50 optional, destinationId, legacyDestinationState, sortKey.
- Labels and category titles are single strings, not localized per EN/BG.
- iconKey is stored but not rendered or edited anywhere.
- No description or image per link.

Approved destinations (domain/approved-destination.ts, portal-settings/portal-approved-destinations-editor.tsx):

- Every link URL must resolve to a Property-scoped Approved Destination.
- Hosts under booking.com, expedia.com, facebook.com, instagram.com, linkedin.com, tripadvisor.com, x.com and youtube.com are auto-approved ('recognized'). Anything else is 'pending' until an AccountAdmin approves it. States: pending, approved, disabled, quarantined.
- A job revalidates every 15 min. Public resolution serves a link only if its destination was validated in the last 30 min.
- Unapproved or lapsed links are silently dropped from the snapshot and from the public response. The server reports this; the guest and manager see nothing.

Admin editing:

- Links tab: LinkTree (link-tree/*) with add/edit/delete category, inline add/edit link (label + URL), dnd reorder of categories and links, and an empty state suggesting a 'Review sites' category.
- The destination request/approve list sits separately in Settings → Guest experience.

Guest rendering and behaviour:

- Links show only after a rating, inside the receipt ('More from {displayName}' → category headings → bordered rows). e2e/critical/guest-portal.spec.ts:76-88 pins that they are hidden before rating, so showing them from arrival is a proposed behaviour change.
- Clicking calls selectSecondaryLinkFn, which records a 'secondary_link' destination selection in the Reporting lifetime aggregate (secondaryLinkSelectionCount, never shown in UI), then assigns the URL.
- The no-JS GET fallback /api/public/p/{token}/click/{linkId} redirects 302 and records no metric.
- A Portal may have zero links.

### Inbox design language

Tokens (src/styles.css):

- Colours are all oklch on a cool 270 hue. Background oklch(0.98 0.003 270) is an off-white page; --surface/--card is white; --surface-elevated. Hairlines --border oklch(0.9…) and --border-strong 0.78. Text is tiered: --text-primary 0.18, --text-secondary/--muted-foreground 0.48, --text-tertiary 0.7.
- One accent: purple --accent/--primary oklch(0.42 0.18 290), hover 0.36, --accent-muted 0.93 for active or selected rows, --ring 0.58.
- Semantic colours: --destructive; --positive/--negative are text-grade, always paired with a sign or arrow; the --warn family (warn, warn-muted, warn-line, warn-track) means 'private/internal' only and is never used alone as a signal; --rating is gold for stars; the chart palette is purple-shifted.
- A full .dark mirror exists, applied by a root THEME_INIT_SCRIPT (light/dark/auto).
- Fonts: Satoshi for body/sans (Fontshare CDN), Plus Jakarta Sans as display (.display-title, not used in the inbox), JetBrains Mono.
- Radius base 6px (--radius 0.375rem): sm 4, md 6, lg 8, xl 12. The composer dock is rounded-xl.

Density and structure (docs/plan/inbox-detail-v2.md, docs/plan/inbox-workspace.md, docs/plan/dashboard-redesign.md):

- Regions are separated by 1px border-b or border-r hairlines, not nested cards. Cards appear only where an edge groups something.
- Header 56px (h-14 border-b), case toolbar 48px (min-h-12, wraps to 85px), list rows fixed at 78px, queue rail 224px (w-56) with 32px rows.
- Meta text is 13px (text-[13px]); secondary is 12px (text-xs muted); 11px uppercase appears only for the rail label; counts use tabular-nums.
- Controls are 32px on desktop and 36px on mobile. The inbox deliberately dropped the 44px rule, citing WCAG 2.5.8's 24px minimum. The dashboard redesign kept ≥44px.
- Dashboard sections are 24px apart, grids 16px; h2 is 18px semibold sentence case (no uppercase eyebrows). PageShell width tiers: 1200 dashboard, 5xl standard, 3xl narrow.

Principles:
(1) Facts, controls and details wear different clothes. A control is an outlined 32px button, with a chevron if it opens a menu, grouped in a ButtonGroup. A fact is plain 13px text with a glyph and no box (CaseFact). A detail is the same text with a dotted underline that opens a popover. Nothing else is a pill; chips are reserved for topics and reply state.
(2) Purple is interactive only. Status facts use a neutral bg-foreground dot; volumes use neutral ink.
(3) Private is said in words plus a dashed edge plus amber, never colour alone.
(4) A ledger/Timeline (ui/timeline.tsx): 32px indicator column, 2px connector, 12px gap, event sentences as actor · verb · object · time. Long runs of system events fold.
(5) One primary action per area. Guarantees are carried by a tooltip and the success toast, not by standing explanatory lines.
(6) Availability renders by exception. Thin data gets a number where there is one, otherwise one sentence plus one action, never a dash. Vocabulary is consistent (Portal scans / Private feedback / Updated).
(7) Search is a mode of the header; bulk selection is the header in another state; empty states name the queue.
(8) Measured, not guessed: stories at 320/390/1440, a metrics gate on geometry, axe, and e2e-pinned accessible names.

The admin should stay neutral and purple, while the guest page carries the property's brand.
