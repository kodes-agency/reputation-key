# Product behavior and capability truth — state of the app, 2026-09-02

## Verdict

The most consequential fact is that the sole live Organization is authorized for 24 of 37 capabilities, yet the repository contains no current, deployed, end-to-end evidence that its Google, reply, AI, notification, or public Guest journeys complete; the latest package ledger still calls the relevant external checks blocked or not started (`docs/operations/capability-state-2026-09-02.md:43-81`; `docs/release-evidence/review/comprehensive-program-status-2026-08-26.json:682-689,731-738,775-780,834-841,885-892,924-931,969-976,1007-1014`). The rewrite did replace many declarations with real call paths: a Google notification can durably become a targeted Review observation and Inbox cycle, reply approval atomically records publication intent before the worker writes and reads back Google, private feedback durably becomes manager work, and Goal results read governed Metric evidence. The Property dashboard nevertheless contradicts the fixed contract in two observable ways: its default is All Time rather than rolling 30 days, and an absent Google-review average is converted to `0` and rendered as `0.0` (`docs/comprehensive-beta-implementation-program-2026-08-25.md:98-105`; `src/routes/_authenticated/properties/$propertyId/index.tsx:17-25`; `src/contexts/review/infrastructure/serving-stats.ts:103-122`; `src/contexts/dashboard/infrastructure/repositories/dashboard.repository.ts:90-104`). The 13 OFF capabilities are mostly honest scope control—four built-and-fenced, six partial, and three identifier-only—and correctly keep registration, secondary Organization creation, competitive recognition, Team, contact/media, upload, permanent erase, and prohibited Google/AI automation out of this beta (`docs/operations/blocked-capabilities-survey-2026-09-02.md:35-51`). However, five legal documents remain drafts with zero approvals and three publication blockers, while a public Guest Portal broadens the real audience beyond the single contracted tenant; issue #377 correctly treats that as an unresolved product boundary (`gh issue view 377 --repo kodes-agency/reputation-key`; `docs/release-evidence/review/comprehensive-progress-report-2026-08-29.md:76-83`). The honest verdict is **a strong, substantially wired source implementation with one lying core surface and an unclosed live-product contract—not yet evidence for a completed closed beta**.

## What was achieved

### PROD-A1 Closed-beta exclusions are executable, not merely documented

**Evidence.** Public registration hits a server-side capability check and redirects to login when denied (`src/routes/register.tsx:15-29`); `/join` requires an invitation identifier and labels the requirement explicitly (`src/routes/join.tsx:22-32,65-67`); the catch-all Better Auth route refuses the raw public sign-up write before Better Auth can accept it (`src/routes/api/auth/$.ts:27-42`). The live truth table records `identity.register`, `organization.create`, custom roles, Team, Badge, Leaderboard, contact, media, upload, permanent erase, and all three prohibited GBP capabilities OFF (`docs/operations/capability-state-2026-09-02.md:49-75,81`).

**Why it matters.** The pre-rewrite concern was false confidence from UI hiding; these refusals sit at route/server execution boundaries and cannot be reopened by stale allowlist rows because compile-time blocking wins (`docs/operations/blocked-capabilities-survey-2026-09-02.md:8-15`).

**Recommendation.** Keep the fail-closed blocks until their product/legal contract changes; delete the three id-only capability identifiers if they are not intended roadmap controls, rather than growing machinery around nonexistent products.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** No immediate fix. Deleting an id-only row is low risk but must update policy rows and operational inventory atomically.

### PROD-A2 The private-rating-first Guest loop is implemented end to end in current source

**Evidence.** The public route resolves a token and dispatches rating/feedback/Google-selection actions (`src/routes/p/$token.tsx:59-71,90-165`). The server reconstructs Portal/session/origin/CSRF authority before execution (`src/contexts/guest/server/public.ts:57-99`); rating submission requires `portal.guest_response` (`src/contexts/guest/server/public.ts:316-376`); private text requires `portal.guest_text` (`src/contexts/guest/server/public.ts:455-499`); Google selection requires an existing rating, `portal.public_read`, and returns the exact stored destination (`src/contexts/guest/server/public.ts:544-596`). The domain accepts only thresholds `1..5`, offers private feedback inclusively at `rating <= threshold`, and persists rating/feedback through the lifecycle (`src/contexts/guest/application/use-cases/guest-response-lifecycle.ts:129-172,496-595`). The UI presents rating first, then Google before optional private feedback (`src/components/guest/guest-response-form-view.tsx:53-77,80-145`). `guest.feedback.submitted` is consumed into an Inbox item/Handling Cycle atomically (`src/contexts/inbox/infrastructure/guest-feedback-outbox-consumers.ts:49-122,193-209`), and the manager server action records explicit handling (`src/contexts/inbox/server/inbox-feedback-handling.ts:20-54`; `src/contexts/inbox/application/use-cases/mark-feedback-handled.ts:34-67`).

**Why it matters.** This closes the earlier gap between a polished Guest form and durable manager follow-through without withholding Google from low raters.

**Recommendation.** Preserve the sequence and exact-destination rules. Capture one live anonymous-browser journey against a published tenant Portal before calling it operationally achieved.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** Evidence capture is low implementation risk; it creates real Guest data and therefore needs the legal/audience gate in PROD-S1 first.

### PROD-A3 Goal Programs now consume governed Metric evidence on a reachable schedule

**Evidence.** Manager Goal routes call `listGoalPrograms`, render create/history controls, and build the result matrix (`src/routes/_authenticated/properties/$propertyId/goals/index.tsx:56-93,95-172`). The worker registers `goal-program.maintain` behind `goal.use` (`src/bootstrap.ts:420-444`), matching the hourly enabled catalogue schedule (`src/shared/governance/event-job-catalogue.ts:2799-2813`). Reconciliation calls `queryGoalMetric` using immutable definition version, subject, and exact period, preserves `updating`, and closes only after a complete source watermark (`src/contexts/goal/application/use-cases/goal-programs.ts:1091-1162`). The dashboard attention query reads only active, open, eligible canonical monthly results and computes pace against elapsed period (`src/contexts/dashboard/infrastructure/adapters/attention-signals.adapter.ts:69-118`). The UI explicitly renders Ready, Updating, Insufficient, or Unavailable with sample, data-through, target, and definition provenance (`src/components/goals/goal-results-matrix.tsx:92-157`).

**Why it matters.** This fixes the pre-rewrite dark Goal event path and avoids layering new product behavior on the old Goal families.

**Recommendation.** Retain the narrow Metric read and canonical monthly model; close obsolete issues #58/#59 or rewrite them against the canonical model so open work does not direct contributors back to retired semantics.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** Issue hygiene is low risk. Physical legacy contraction remains a separate, evidence-gated operation.

**Why it matters.** This is load-bearing: a process crash, duplicate delivery, stale draft, ambiguous provider response, or external Google edit cannot be mistaken for a confirmed publication.

**Recommendation.** Keep this machinery. Do not simplify the provider-truth or atomic-intent boundaries; simplify surrounding catalogues instead.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** No code fix recommended. A live provider canary has side effects and must use an authorized non-customer profile.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** No current fix. Comparator changes are high migration/replay risk and need shadow comparison.

### PROD-G3 Missing first-party Metric evidence is modeled honestly in Goal and most Dashboard paths

## What should be improved

### PROD-I1 The ON Dashboard journey violates both reporting defaults and missing-data truth

**Evidence.** The contract requires rolling 30 days versus the preceding 30 and forbids coercing missing/insufficient data to zero (`docs/comprehensive-beta-implementation-program-2026-08-25.md:98-105`). The Property route defaults `timeRange` to `all`, while only Google performance defaults to `30d` (`src/routes/_authenticated/properties/$propertyId/index.tsx:17-25`). Review serving stats converts SQL `avg(...) = null` to `0` (`src/contexts/review/infrastructure/serving-stats.ts:103-122`), and Dashboard carries that non-null scalar into value/prior/trend (`src/contexts/dashboard/infrastructure/repositories/dashboard.repository.ts:38-50,90-104`). Metric-backed scans/feedback, by contrast, preserve `null` and availability (`src/contexts/dashboard/infrastructure/repositories/dashboard.repository.ts:52-87`).

**Why it matters.** A manager with no eligible Google reviews is told the Property has a `0.0` reputation, and the first Property view silently changes the agreed question from “last 30 days” to “all history.” This is a visible false business fact on an ON core capability, so severity is high under this review's rule.

**Recommendation.** Change the Property search default to `30d`; make Review period stats carry `avgRating: number | null` plus evidence; suppress comparison/trend unless both periods are non-null; render the same `Updating/Insufficient/Unavailable` vocabulary already used by Metric/Goal. Do not patch the JSX with `count === 0`—fix the source type and all consumers.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** Small-to-medium. It changes a shared DTO/repository contract and cache keys but no storage; the main risk is callers that currently assume arithmetic scalars.

### PROD-I2 Capability refusal is technically correct but product-indistinguishable

**Evidence.** `CapabilitySet` exposes only `allowed: string[]` (`src/shared/auth/capability-set.ts:34-42,50-61`). The controlled route gate redirects to `/unavailable` with a feature identifier only (`src/shared/auth/controlled-route-gate.ts:11-21,29-35`), and navigation presents the same “Not available in this beta” treatment for denied items (`src/components/layout/manager-nav-items.tsx:39-78,94-105`; `src/components/layout/nav-items-shared.tsx:18`). Issue #413 records the resulting ambiguity between wrong audience, incomplete implementation, tenant not allowlisted, and temporarily unavailable (`gh issue view 413 --repo kodes-agency/reputation-key`).

**Why it matters.** The sole beta operator receives no action or owner: invite/permission problems, deliberate beta exclusions, legal blocks, and service readiness all look identical. That turns accurate backend refusal into support load and makes the 24/37 state unusable as a product promise.

**Recommendation.** Keep detailed policy internals server-side, but return a stable user-facing category and next action: `not_in_beta`, `needs_admin_enablement`, `needs_google_connection`, `needs_ai_authorization`, `temporarily_unavailable`, or `wrong_role`. Add categories to the route response and disabled-nav presentation; do not expose raw policy row/reason text.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** Medium and bounded. The risk is leaking tenant policy or creating a second authorization decision; prevent that by deriving copy only after the authoritative deny and never using the category to grant access.

### PROD-I3 The reviewed source and installed capability approval are not one frozen product state

**Evidence.** The current tree is `dc1fc97e3f33dafdf08b22259989d17d7b6bf80a`, while the capability-state record binds approval to `5fafeae95fbe951038af31cc143c388ccc116060`; `git rev-list --count 5fafeae95fbe951038af31cc143c388ccc116060..HEAD` returned `13`. Those 13 commits include Review targeted-read/publication fixes and Property behavior (`git log --oneline 5fafeae95fbe951038af31cc143c388ccc116060..HEAD`). This does **not** prove the deployed runtime is stale; the deployed digest is not in the reviewed evidence.

**Why it matters.** We can review current source or an approved/deployed product, but cannot claim they are the same without the deployment manifest. In particular, two post-binding Review fixes affect the exact reply-observation loop being assessed.

**Recommendation.** Freeze one current SHA, bind Google/capability approvals and image digests to it, deploy those bytes, and capture the three journeys below against that release. Until then, label current-source and live-tenant claims separately.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** Operationally bounded but potentially blocking: a promotion/readback cycle, not new feature code.

## What needs substantial change

### PROD-S1 Public Guest and AI audience obligations remain undecided while their platform capabilities are ON

**Evidence.** The latest progress report records five legal documents, five drafts, zero approvals, and three publication blockers (`docs/release-evidence/review/comprehensive-progress-report-2026-08-29.md:76-83`). Issue #377 states that Google reviewer data is third-party data and that a Guest Portal makes the real audience broader than the one participant, then asks what the closed beta is permitted to show (`gh issue view 377 --repo kodes-agency/reputation-key`). The fixed contract requires authorized/disclosed AI transfer and counsel approval (`docs/comprehensive-beta-implementation-program-2026-08-25.md:90-96`), while the live policy resolves all three AI capabilities and the three core Guest capabilities ON (`docs/operations/capability-state-2026-09-02.md:46-48,67-70`). The AI package record still says provider region/retention/legal facts and deployed-cell evidence are blocked (`docs/release-evidence/review/comprehensive-program-status-2026-08-26.json:885-892,969-976`).

**Why it matters.** This is not copy polish. It determines whether the real user can lawfully enable AI and whether anonymous guests/reviewers can be exposed to the current notices and processing. If downstream legal gates refuse correctly, ON overstates availability; if they do not, the product is reachable before its audience contract is approved.

**Recommendation.** Decide the audience first: enumerate manager, invited user, guest respondent, and Google reviewer surfaces; obtain external-counsel approval for the exact deployed documents/data uses; bind approved bytes to the release; keep AI authorization and public Portal publication fail-closed until those checks pass. If counsel approval will not arrive for this cohort, descoped replacement: run manager-only beta with Portal publication and all AI capabilities unavailable, while retaining internal first-party Portal configuration/Metric/Goal work.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** High coordination, low code if current gates exist. The risk of skipping it is unauthorized processing or misleading consent/notice; the risk of waiting is a narrower beta, which is acceptable for one tenant.

### PROD-S2 Replace “capability ON” as a release headline with journey readiness, not another catalogue

**Evidence.** Policy truth says 24 ON (`docs/operations/capability-state-2026-09-02.md:29-34`), while the latest package evidence calls Google, Review lifecycle, reply publication, AI, and hosted Inbox verification incomplete (`docs/release-evidence/review/comprehensive-program-status-2026-08-26.json:682-689,731-738,775-780,834-841,885-892`). **Why it matters.** An authorization decision answers “may this execute?”; a product readiness statement answers “can this user complete the promised outcome now?” Treating the first as the second recreates the pre-rewrite false-confidence problem.

**Recommendation — minimal ordered change set.** (1) Fix PROD-I1 before showing Dashboard data. (2) Freeze and identify the exact deployed SHA/digests. (3) Resolve PROD-S1 and bind the approved legal bytes. (4) For each currently ON external journey, either retain the live proof named below or make the surface unavailable with a specific reason; do not broaden capability scope. (5) Capture Google notification→Inbox, Confirm & Publish→Google observation, Guest rating/feedback→handled, Notification Bell/email, and AI enable/use/erase on the frozen release. (6) Only then describe those capabilities as available to the cohort. Store one compact release manifest with pointers to evidence; do **not** add a second 6,000-line catalogue.

**Cost/risk of the fix.** One release/evidence cycle plus two bounded UI/domain fixes. It can reduce the advertised beta temporarily; that is safer and cheaper than accepting an unproved external side effect.

## Opinion (clearly separated, short)

The durable Google/Reply and Guest/Inbox boundaries are the right kind of complexity; they protect external side effects and private data. The product is nevertheless over-instrumented for one Organization when 9,392 catalogue lines and 116 gates can coexist with no retained proof of the three journeys a beta participant experiences. The next marginal week should delete ambiguity and run the product, not add another authority, catalogue, gate, or compatibility model.
