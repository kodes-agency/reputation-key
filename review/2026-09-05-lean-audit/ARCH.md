## What it missed or got wrong under the one-developer lens

- **The contraction gates are self-imposed, not real.** `CTX.md:260` treats "export/restore proof, one verified release without a compatibility reader, a reversible migration" as hard blockers. No external party requires any of it: the Google response (`docs/product-readiness-program-2026-07/google-business-profile-ai-policy-response-2026-07-14.md:30,83`) constrains raw review content only, and none of the 14 dark tables holds it. A `pg_dump` of those tables to the evidence store is the whole retention obligation. Meanwhile "controlled contraction" is costing 91 governance rows, 6,286 test lines, 36 commits in 30 days, `team.repository.ts`/`team-membership.repository.ts` (597 lines of CRUD with an empty build at `src/contexts/team/build.ts:9-16`), and `badge.awarded` still wired into notification templates and the trigger matrix (`src/contexts/notification/domain/notification-templates.ts:391`, `src/contexts/notification/application/beta-notification-trigger-matrix.ts:213`) for an event with no producer.
- **"The issue is not 17 contexts for six properties" (`CTX.md:264`) is wrong for one maintainer.** The boundaries themselves are the cost: 139 port files / 12,927 lines, 51 of 85 `*Port` interfaces with exactly one implementation, 108 adapters / 29,399 lines, and 16 production files to touch for one event (`review.reply.published`). The review's fix (an executable table-ownership rule with `(importer, table, mode, reason, expiry)` allowlist entries, `CTX.md:226`) adds a catalogue; merging the contexts that share data removes the edges.
- **The outbox is fine; the in-process bus is the waste.** `ARCH.md:259` correctly calls the outbox load-bearing, but recommends fixing the cutover state machine per family (`ARCH.md:166`). The cheaper answer is that the bus is a second delivery path for the same facts: `src/shared/events/event-bus.ts` (112), `emit-and-record.ts` (44), `cutover-flags.ts:5-18`, `shadow-compare.ts:1-3` (132), and 3,714 lines of bus handlers, while durable consumers already exist for 39 event types. Cross-context durable edges are exactly 7 pairs; two of them (review→inbox, portal→metric) are same-database projections that can be written in the source transaction with no event at all.
- **Three deployable containers is more ceremony, not less.** `ARCH.md:210` recommends exact `WebContainer`/`WorkerContainer`/`OperatorContainer` types. For one process pair, one `createContainer(overrides)` plus "the worker calls `registerJobs`" is the whole distinction; `src/composition/deployables.ts` (196), `container-partition.ts` (147), their tests (519) and `process-fixtures/*` exist to prove a boundary nobody crosses.
- **Region routing for a one-cell beta was not priced.** ADR 0057 fixes a single US cell, yet 6,557 prod + 6,367 test lines of data-cell/region-move machinery remain (`src/shared/db/single-us-data-cell-cutover.ts`, `src/shared/routing/data-cell-execution-fence.ts`, `src/contexts/property/domain/region-move-workflow.ts`). This is also the mechanism that makes a non-US property vanish from the UI.
- **ADR 0045's three-way separation is not what the code does.** `audit_logs` is written from `src/contexts/goal/infrastructure/repositories/goal-program.repository.ts:332` and `governed-goal.repository.ts`, not from a "security/audit module"; the review did not notice the third concept has no owner.
- **Test weight was not counted as maintenance cost.** In 13 of 17 contexts tests outnumber production (inbox 26,933 vs 19,393; goal 14,618 vs 12,208); `src/shared/architecture` is 908 prod against 10,033 test lines that assert repository shape.

## The one thing to do first

Delete `team`, `badge`, and `leaderboard` outright this week (row 1): `pg_dump` the 17 tables into `docs/release-evidence/`, drop them in one migration, delete the three directories, the three ops scripts, the in-memory team repo, the `badge.awarded` rows in notification, and the 91 governance mentions, and mark ADRs 0013/0014/0021/0043 retired in the same commit. It is the cheapest cut (two days, builds already empty, no external obligation beyond keeping the dump), it removes the touchpoints every later deletion would otherwise have to re-edit (`capability-fate.ts:107-122`, `beta-capabilities.ts`, `data-fate-authority.ts`, `migratable.ts`, three catalogues), and it settles the governing question for the whole programme: whether a "controlled contraction" gate that no external party imposes can hold up a deletion. Once that precedent is set, rows 2-4 (bus, catalogues, region machinery) remove roughly 35k more lines with the same argument and no product change.
